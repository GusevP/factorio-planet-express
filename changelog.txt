Version: 2.1.9
Date: 2026-08-15
  Bugfixes:
    - Ships no longer turn around in mid-flight and shuttle back and forth without ever delivering. While a ship was flying a two-way trade, the fleet re-checks how much the destination can still spare and lowers the load if it has dropped — and rewriting the ship's orders to match silently reset which stop it was heading for, sending it back to where it came from. It then re-requested the outbound cargo and set off again, looping indefinitely while appearing to be in transit. The active stop is now preserved, and the ship keeps flying without so much as a change in speed.

Version: 2.1.4
Date: 2026-08-05
  Bugfixes:
    - Platforms no longer cut their engines part-way through a long flight. The watchdog that frees genuinely stuck ships only counted progress when a ship reached a new stop — and a ship crossing between planets does not reach one for the whole leg, so any flight longer than the watchdog's window was mistaken for a stall: its orders were cancelled mid-space, it drifted to a halt with the cargo still aboard, and it was marked "stuck" until the next dispatch picked it up again. Movement now counts as progress, so a flight of any length is left alone while a ship that is truly going nowhere still times out.
    - A ship is no longer cancelled at the instant it departs its second-to-last stop. Its schedule advances to the final stop the moment the loading condition clears, while the platform is still at rest building up speed, and the fleet read that as "arrived, job done" — clearing the route and stranding the load it had just taken on. Arrival is now confirmed against the planet the platform is actually at.
  Features:
    - The fleet monitor now says WHY a ship is stuck. Hover the "stuck" label on any roster row for the cause read straight from the platform: out of thruster fuel, no route to its destination, paused, a wait condition that never cleared, or held by the ready signal.

Version: 1.8.0
Date: 2026-06-22
  Features:
    - The fleet no longer lets a short, busy route hog every freed ship while a far planet with a huge backlog waits forever. Each planet now tracks how long it has gone unserved, and when ships are scarce the one that has waited longest wins the next ship — so a starved planet (say a distant Gleba with hundreds of outstanding requests) climbs the queue instead of being shut out by a fast Vulcanus-to-Nauvis science loop. The order is fully deterministic and survives save/load and multiplayer.
    - New "Minimum load" setting (runtime-global, default 80%): a ship now waits to fill at least this fraction of its cargo before it dispatches, instead of burning a whole trip on a tiny partial load. Demand that can't fill a ship yet accumulates at the source and ships once it batches into a worthwhile load. Set it to 0 to ship any load immediately (the old behaviour). Perishable cargo and trips that would otherwise leave a ship idle are always exempt, so nothing rots or strands waiting to batch.
  Changes:
    - Lowering "Max ships per route" is no longer the recommended way to spread ships across planets — the new anti-starvation ordering distributes a scarce fleet fairly on its own.

    - First public release.
